# SUS Usability Survey — Speech-to-FSL Browser Extension

Based on the standard 10-item System Usability Scale (Jordan &
Thomas, 1996 — already in your reference list as [6]/[19] across
chapters). This is the same instrument your methodology (Ch. 3,
"Usability Evaluation Model") commits to using.

## Instructions for administering

- Give this **after** the participant has used the extension for a
  short, guided task (e.g., "have a short conversation while the
  extension is running and observe the FSL output for at least 3
  phrases").
- Each item is rated on a 5-point scale: **1 = Strongly Disagree**
  to **5 = Strongly Agree**.
- Odd-numbered items are worded positively, even-numbered items
  negatively — this is intentional in the original SUS instrument
  (it discourages straight-line answering). Do not "fix" the
  negative wording.
- Standard SUS scoring: for odd items, score = (rating − 1); for
  even items, score = (5 − rating). Sum all 10, multiply by 2.5.
  Result is a 0–100 score. (This conversion is what Eq. in your
  methodology should reference alongside the weighted mean, if not
  already there.)

## The 10 items

1. I think that I would like to use this FSL translation extension frequently.
2. I found the extension unnecessarily complex.
3. I thought the extension was easy to use.
4. I think that I would need the support of a technical person to be able to use this extension.
5. I found the various functions in this extension were well integrated.
6. I thought there was too much inconsistency in this extension.
7. I would imagine that most people would learn to use this extension very quickly.
8. I found the extension very cumbersome/awkward to use.
9. I felt very confident using the extension.
10. I needed to learn a lot of things before I could get going with this extension.

## Supplementary items (optional, not part of standard SUS scoring)

These aren't scored into the SUS total, but they map directly onto
your stated evaluation criteria (accuracy, responsiveness, usability)
and Objective 4, and are worth including as a separate section so you
have qualitative material for Chapter 4/5 discussion:

11. The FSL sign videos matched what I expected for the words I spoke. (1–5)
12. The delay between speaking and seeing the sign felt acceptable. (1–5)
13. When the extension did not have a sign for a word, it was clear that a sign was missing (rather than the extension seeming broken). (1–5)
14. I would recommend this extension to someone who communicates with deaf or hard-of-hearing individuals. (1–5)
15. Open-ended: What was the most confusing or frustrating part of using the extension?
16. Open-ended: What would make this more useful for you?
